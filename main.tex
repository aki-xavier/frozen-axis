\begin{abstract}%
Many inference tasks share a common difficulty: local observations do not suffice to determine the structure being sought, context must be brought in, and how strongly context should be used varies with position and time. Real-time 3D reconstruction, image restoration, and online state estimation all belong to this class, which this paper calls open inversion tasks. The class is delimited by four criteria: observations are ill-posed, the required context strength is heterogeneous and content-addressed by the evidence, inference inputs are not constrained by the training distribution, and the demand rule cannot be given within any pre-given tolerance on the open set by any values frozen before inference begins. This paper proves that no single fixed-memory component can complete such tasks on its own. A fixed-memory component is one whose context rules are frozen into parameters that do not change at inference time and whose values come from a training corpus or from manual setting; neural networks (NN) and Bayesian networks (BN) are its two canonical instances. The argument unfolds along a decision axis: the content of a context rule is a set of numbers, and numbers have only two possible sources. Values given on the spot by current evidence or by analytic invariants are called Mode A; values given offline from training-corpus statistics or manual setting and frozen at inference time are called Mode B. The two are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, because a number either comes into existence at inference time or already exists before inference begins, and there is no third state. Generalization on the open set requires rules of Mode A, while the rules of a fixed-memory component belong to Mode B, and the falsification closes at this point. The exclusion does not rest on the quadratic smoothing form of the classical variational problem: it holds for any separable data term with any even differentiable pairwise potential, and the minimal obstruction to a frozen coefficient family is one single position whose required strength ratio changes between two admissible configurations, whatever number of entries such a family has. Bayesian networks have two further walls of different status beyond the negation: the absence of a perceptual proposal layer, which is the negation read at the input interface rather than an independent theorem, and treewidth blow-up when the source of structure is missing, an external hardness result orthogonal to the negation. What is falsified is solo use rather than the components themselves: high accuracy on closed benchmarks is a weak claim and is fully compatible with our conclusion. All derivations are formal and rely on no experimental data. The algebraic skeleton of the argument chain has been machine-verified with Lean 4 and Mathlib, with zero \texttt{sorry} and zero warnings; the carrier-independent algebraic core and the structured general forms are machine-proved at general types, the theorems on the toy carrier are special cases of them, and the remaining modeling components on the continuous side are listed explicitly in Section~\ref{ch:verify}.
\end{abstract}
