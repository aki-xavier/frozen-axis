\section{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

For a fixed test distribution and squared rule-value loss, a fixed evidence-instantiated benchmark, and a specified offline-frozen class satisfying uniform BD, P4a, and scale-restricted P4b, a positive $\kappa = p\delta^2/16-\rho$ excludes uniform $\epsilon$-accuracy throughout that class for every $0<\epsilon<\kappa$. This is a conditional result about independent task solving, not a denial of in-distribution usefulness, not logic completeness, and not a consequence of P1--P3 alone. The decision axis explains the conditions: ill-posedness forces context; Lemma~1 excludes the global scalar, and Lemma~2 separates objective form from provenance; Criterion P4 and Lemma~3 supply the quantitative argument; Proposition~4 classifies the collision case; Theorem~5 closes only under the stated class, benchmark, and tolerance hypotheses. If the comparison class exhausts the offline-frozen Mode~B alternatives satisfying those hypotheses, an admitted rule meeting the bound must be evidence-instantiated; Mode~A membership alone is not sufficient for correctness. The amortization gap records the empirical Mode~B gap, No-Free-Lunch the algorithm-level universal negation; between them this paper supplies a conditional component-level boundary, a unified decision axis, and, against No-Free-Lunch, a qualified converse on open inversion tasks.

Beyond neural networks the classification reaches Markov random fields, conditional random fields, Gaussian mixture models, dictionary learning, kernel methods and others whose context rules are carried by offline-trained or hand-set parameters; the quantitative exclusion applies to them only when their rules belong to a specified class satisfying uniform BD and P4, and random fields with coupling strengths computed online from current evidence are Mode~A, outside the negation. The boundary: tasks whose demand is exactly identifiable from a fixed corpus are outside the class, where a fixed-memory component can be complete; the falsification stops where the demand becomes freezable. The stationarity obstruction is independent of the quadratic smoothing form: Lemma~1 holds for a separable data term, any even differentiable pairwise potential and no convexity; its minimal obstruction is an equivalence, not a sufficient condition---one position whose required strength ratio changes between two admissible configurations. This excludes every frozen coefficient family, however large; the global scalar, requiring the ratio to be common to all positions, is the shallowest member of the excluded class, not a special case defended separately.

Negation is not disparagement: their closed-benchmark achievements are genuine; only the universal claim ``solo use is complete'' is negated, not their usefulness in combinations, and Mode A's engineering implementation, the decision axis's other side, is left to future work.
