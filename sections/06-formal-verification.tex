\section{Formal Verification}
\label{ch:verify}

\subsection{Object and Method of Verification}

The algebraic skeleton of the argument chain has been machine-verified with Lean 4 and Mathlib, fully implemented with zero \texttt{sorry} and zero warnings; Lean 4 has become an active platform at the intersection of machine-assisted proof and machine learning \citep{yang2023}. Let the wording first be delimited: ``formal falsification'' in the title refers to the deductive method itself, namely that all conclusions are derived formally from definitions and lemmas; what machine verification guarantees is the algebraic form of this deductive chain on the toy carrier, while the universal claim is still borne by the prose deduction. The verification covers, on the Lean side, Definitions 1 to 4, Lemmas 1 to 3, Propositions 4 and 6, and Theorems 5 and 7, and the final theorem \texttt{finale} composes the generalization side and the carrier side into a single conjunction. The formalization adopts a toy carrier: the position set has four positions, structure fields are functions from positions to reals, pixel 0 plays the evidence position, and pixel 1 plays the context position. The role of the toy carrier is not to simulate a real task but to make every algebraic step of the argument chain machine-checkable.

The verification follows a completeness principle: every premise not explicitly given in the paper is explicitly included as a premise parameter of the theorem, and nothing is implicitly assumed. This principle directly shapes the verified form of Lemma 3, detailed below. The guaranteed scope of the artifact is exactly the guarantee-tier table of Section~\ref{sec:layers}. Universal claims beyond that scope, for example the satisfaction of Criterion P4 on real tasks, are not covered by the machine verification. The sites where the verified premises differ from the prose, namely that P4 appears as the parameterized premise $g(R) > \epsilon$ rather than as a formalized criterion, are stated explicitly in the next section.

\subsection{Three Levels of Verification Strength}

The verified content divides into three levels of strength.

The strongest level is complete deduction, with conclusions and proofs free of extra premises. This level includes: the strengthened form of Lemma 1, where a single global scalar at an arbitrary fitting point forces residuals and consistency terms to be proportional across positions, namely \eqref{eq:proportion}, and failure of the proportionality is contradiction, the proof being merely two substitutions and one commutativity; the two-sided form of Lemma 2, where Paradigm E with a frozen scalar coefficient has no nonzero stationary point at a fitting point under heterogeneity, while the objective of Paradigm I always has a unique minimizer, minimality coming from nonnegativity of the sum of squares and uniqueness from the fact that the sum of squares vanishes only termwise; the classification of Propositions 4 and 6, with the proof that fixed-parameter rules do not belong to Mode A unfolding line by line along the collision argument of Proposition 4; and the closure of Theorems 5 and 7.

The next level is the landing of the criteria. Ill-posedness is formalized as the observation mask having a hole, and both directions of the mask criterion are machine-provable: a hole implies ill-posedness by constructive bifurcation, and a full mask implies unique determination by position-wise pinning. The open-set Criterion P3 constructs the collision sample pair with evidence pinned and context freely bifurcating: take two structure fields that take the observed value at evidence positions and two different numbers at context positions; both fields agree with the observation on the mask. That P3 implies P1 is thereby also provable. The collision pair feeds Theorems 5 and 7, and the heterogeneity Criterion P2 is consumed at Lemmas 1 and 2.

The weakest level is premise parameterization, which falls on Lemma 3. The toy form lists upper-tail realization, a risk gap exceeding the bound, and nonnegativity of the minimum risk all explicitly as theorem premises; in the general measure form, upper-tail realization is given by Mathlib's first-moment-method theorem and is no longer a premise, leaving only a risk gap exceeding the bound and nonnegativity of the minimum risk as explicit premises. The verification side parameterizes the risk-gap premise as $g(R) > \epsilon$, aligned with the quantified form of Criterion P4 at the definitional site in Section~\ref{ch:falsify}. The conclusion is that the generalization bound cannot hold, and the proof is exactly the four-line derivation of Lemma 3. Among these, the risk gap exceeding the bound is demonstrated in mechanism by the toy instance: the total squared loss of a Mode B rule fitting the target $t(d) = d_0 \cdot d_1$ on the three-point open-set samples $(1, 0)$, $(0, 1)$, $(1, 1)$ has the positive lower bound $1$, the total squared loss of an evidence-only rule $R(d) = \Phi(d_0)$ has the lower bound $1/2$ attained by $R(d) = d_0/2$, and the class gap $1/2$ is positive, both sides decidable directly by nonlinear arithmetic. The toy's demand depends on the context coordinate and is not computable from evidence, so the toy demonstrates the gap mechanism rather than the completeness of Mode A; the in-class demonstration is given in Section~\ref{ch:falsify}.

\subsection{The Final Theorem and the Scope Statement}

The final theorem \texttt{finale} composes the two sides into one conjunction: under Criterion P3 and the measure-theoretic premises of Lemma 3, a rule carried by fixed parameters neither belongs to Mode A nor can satisfy the generalization bound on the open set. The carrier side answers ``where the rule comes from'', and the generalization side answers ``what goes wrong with the rule on unseen inputs''; together they are the algebraic form of the negation of ``solo use is complete''. The guaranteed scope of the verification is given component by component in the next section.

\subsection{Correspondence from the Toy Carrier to the Continuous Case}
\label{sec:layers}

A blanket ``from toy to real'' declaration leaves the reader with a question of trust; this section upgrades it to a component-wise guarantee. Every argument component is assigned by guarantee strength to one of three tiers. Tier A is the carrier-independent algebraic core, whose proofs cite no property of the carrier and can be machine-proved directly at general types; the toy end and the continuous end are both mere instantiations. Tier B is structured general forms, which require the structure of measure spaces, inner product spaces, or modules, for which Mathlib already provides anchors, and which are machine-provable up to the level of general structures. Tier C is the remaining modeling components, belonging to derivations or correspondences on the continuous side, not covered by the machine guarantee and listed explicitly item by item. Three remarks. First, the existence of Tier A is itself a mathematical fact rather than a defense: the proof of Lemma 1 consists of only two substitutions and one commutativity, and its content is a proportionality identity holding for arbitrary reals; classification and closure likewise consume only the property that values differ on collision pairs and hold over arbitrary index types. Second, Tier B pushes the distribution-related components onto structure theorems of Mathlib. Upper-tail realization is the first moment method, holding universally for any probability measure and integrable loss; it is demoted from an explicit premise of Lemma 3 to a theorem, and the explicit premises of the general form of Lemma 3 reduce to two, namely a risk gap exceeding the bound and nonnegativity of the minimum risk; the unique minimizer of Paradigm I is given by the positive definiteness of inner product spaces, with the conclusion in the measure form being almost-everywhere equality. The reduction of Lemma 3's premises from three to two is the one step in this upgrade where the machine genuinely crosses the boundary between toy and general. Third, Tier C has only two items: the derivation of first-order optimality conditions on the continuous side, namely the Euler--Lagrange equation turning the variational objective into pointwise form together with the correspondence between the consistency coefficients and the corresponding differential operator; and whether networks are nondegenerate on collision inputs, a property checkable for concrete systems, which this paper neither assumes nor machine-proves.

The scope statement is thereby precise to the component. Machine verification guarantees all of Tier A and the structured forms of Tier B: the algebraic core is machine-proved at general types, and the measure and inner-product-space parts are anchored on Mathlib theorems. The two modeling components of Tier C are not covered by the machine guarantee: the continuous-side variational derivation belongs to standard calculus-of-variations citation, and network nondegeneracy is a checkable property. The universal property that Mode A and Mode B are mutually exclusive and exhaustive remains a definition-level argument; see Section~\ref{ch:axis}. In other words, what Lean verifies is no longer merely that the toy skeleton has no breakpoints, but that every carrier-independent part of the skeleton is machine-proved at general types; the flesh-and-blood correspondence from the skeleton to real systems is compressed into the two explicit claims of Tier C.

\subsection{Artifact Availability}

The Lean artifact is published together with the paper in the \texttt{lean-proof} directory of the project repository at \texttt{https://github.com/aki-xavier/frozen-axis}: a self-contained Lake project containing the development file \texttt{FormalProof.lean}, the \texttt{lakefile.lean} and \texttt{lake-manifest.json} that pin Mathlib and every transitive dependency to an exact revision, and the \texttt{lean-toolchain} file that pins the toolchain to \texttt{leanprover/lean4:v4.34.0}. The reproduction command is \texttt{lake build}, run from inside that directory. The development file is 699 lines and contains all definitions, lemmas, propositions, theorems, the final theorem, and the generalization upgrades; its content is identified by the blob hash \texttt{71609596}, so the verified object can be fixed independently of any later revision of the repository.

Three properties of the build are worth stating in the paper itself rather than leaving to inspection. First, the build completes with zero errors and zero warnings. Second, the development contains no \texttt{sorry} and no \texttt{axiom} declaration of its own. Third, and strongest, the axiom budget of the load-bearing declarations is Lean's own standard set: \texttt{\#print axioms} applied to \texttt{finale}, \texttt{lemma1\_sharp}, and \texttt{tail\_realization} returns \texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]} in each case, a list containing no \texttt{sorryAx}, so no step of the chain rests on an unproved assertion. The artifact is therefore auditable at the level of the proof term rather than only at the level of a reported build outcome.

Appendix~\ref{app:artifact} makes the verification inspectable without access to the artifact. It records the layout and the reproduction command, and it gives two things a reader needs in order to judge the claim rather than take it on trust: an index from every paper item (Definitions 1 to 4, the mask criterion, Criteria P1 to P4, Lemmas 1 to 3, Propositions 4 and 6, Theorems 5 and 7, and the final theorem) to the Lean declaration that discharges it, carrying the guarantee tier of Table~\ref{tab:layers}; and the machine-checked statements of the load-bearing declarations with their premises written out in full, so that the premise list of \texttt{finale} can be compared line by line against the prose of Section~\ref{ch:falsify}. The comparison exposes the single point at which the verified form is deliberately weaker than the prose: Criterion P4 enters the development as the premise parameter \texttt{hGapPositive}, not as a formalized criterion, exactly as announced above. Every other premise of the chain is either proved outright or listed explicitly in the statement of the theorem that consumes it.
